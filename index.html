Social Security & Medicare
Public pension instead of Social Security
Teachers in fifteen states, and a lot of police, fire and municipal work, are outside Social Security altogether: nothing is withheld for it, a set percentage of pay goes to the pension fund instead, and Medicare is still taken. Illinois TRS is 9%.
Overtime tax break (federal law, 2025–2028)
Only the premium half of overtime pay counts — the extra half-rate on each OT hour, not the whole thing. Capped at $12,500 a year single / $25,000 married. Federal income tax only — Social Security, Medicare and state tax still apply.
Fixed deductions, per paycheck
The same dollars every check — union dues, a 401k loan payment, child support, a health premium. What each one comes out before matters: a health plan comes out before every tax including Social Security, while a 401k comes out before income tax only — Social Security and Medicare are still charged on it.
Percentage deductions
Anything taken as a share of your pay rather than a fixed sum — a 401k or 403b contribution, a pension. Entered as a percentage because that is how payroll assesses it: it moves with every overtime hour and every raise, where a dollar figure typed in once is wrong by the next check.

        Jobs
        

      

        
A second job is a whole separate set of terms — its own rate,
        schedule, overtime rule, holidays and differentials — so each one keeps its own
        settings and its own clock. Overtime is never combined across employers: forty hours
        at one and ten at another is fifty straight hours, not ten of overtime. Tax is the
        part that combines, because it is charged to you rather than to them.

      

      

      

        
      

      

        

      

      

        Your contract
        

      

        
The dates are the ones on your contract — the first and last day you are
        required to be there, not the first and last day of classes. Everything else is worked out
        from them.

        Pay period & payday
        

      

        

        

        

        

          

      

      

        
The anchor is any date a period started on — periods repeat from it forever, so this never needs touching again.
        Figures are gross (before taxes and deductions). Everything is stored only in this browser.

      

        

      

      

        Your schedule
        

      

        
The hours you are rostered for. Everything that asks "how far outside
        your shift was this" — the decimal time card, the what-if at the clock — measures
        against these.

      

      

        

        

      

      

        
The roster the app judges everything else against. "The day before a
        holiday" means the shift before it, not whatever date happens to sit there — so a
        Monday holiday looks at Sunday and Tuesday, and one on a Friday reaches back to
        Thursday and forward to Sunday.

      

      

        
        
        
      

      

      

        
        
Most agreements with a fixed roster pay premium for a day off you
        gave up, however short it is. Your overtime rule cannot see it on its own — five hours
        on a day off is under every threshold there is, so it would otherwise be paid as
        ordinary time.

      

        

          

        

          

      

      
Lunch starts after 5 worked hours — 
      the clock pauses for it mid-shift, shifts already logged are corrected automatically, and lunch
      time never counts toward overtime. 'Extra' is only for lunches hiding in hours the log cannot
      see, like a year-so-far carry-in — logged shifts are already handled.

      

        
A shift that crosses midnight is still one shift, and it belongs to
        one day. By default that is whichever day holds most of it — so a 10 PM start is the
        next day's shift, while a 6 PM start is that evening's. Change it only if your
        employer names them some other way.

      

      

        

          

      

      

        
Some contracts only pay overtime once you have actually worked your
        scheduled hours — miss a day and you work back out of it before overtime starts
        again. Turn this on and hours past your daily threshold pay the hole down first;
        only what is left over pays at the overtime rate. It settles the whole window, so
        calling off on Wednesday takes back Monday's overtime — though not for nothing,
        since those hours go straight onto the hole. Applies to the daily and per-shift
        rules only: a weekly or pay-period threshold already handles a missed day by never
        reaching forty. You are never paid less for hours you worked.

      

      

        

          

        

          

      

      

      

      

        
Time clocks drift, and the machine at work is the one that prints
        your card. If it reads a minute or two off your phone, turn this on and the app shows
        the times the machine would stamp — so what you copy onto a slip matches what it
        printed. Your hours and your pay do not change: both ends of a shift move together.
        Leave it off until you have watched the difference hold for a few days.

      

      

        

          

        

          

      

      

        

      

      

        Premiums
        

      

        
Extra money per hour for the part of a shift inside a window — the
        night or evening premium a lot of contracts pay. It is paid on the hours that fall
        inside it, not on the whole shift, and it rides on your base rate, so overtime is
        worked out on rate plus differential. Paid days off never earn it.

One differential is enough for most jobs. Some pay several at once
        — nights, weekends, charge — and they add rather than replace, so an hour can carry
        all of them. Leave the times blank for a premium that applies to the whole shift,
        which is what charge pay usually is. Paid days off never earn any of them.

      

      

      

        
      

      


      

        
Being called in usually pays a guaranteed minimum however short the
        job is — twenty minutes at 3 AM bills the contract's minimum. Those hours count as
        hours worked, so they push you toward overtime like any others. Tick a shift as a
        call-in when you log it.

      

      

        

          

      

        

      

      

        Time off
        

      

        
Days off that still pay. Each one is a rule rather than a date, so
        the calendar is right every year without anyone editing it. Switch one off if your
        contract does not have it, rename it, or add your own.

      

      

        

          

        

          

        

          

      

      

      

        
        
      

      
A standard set replaces whatever is listed now.
      Federal is what the Postal Service and most federal employers observe. Anything your
      contract adds — Good Friday, the day after Thanksgiving, your own — you add above.

      

        
Allowances you spend rather than days that arrive — floating
        holidays, sick days, personal days, whatever your contract calls them. Set how many
        a year, what each is worth, and whether they count toward overtime. Name them
        individually if it helps to know which you have left.

      

      

      

        
        
        
      

      

        
A block of dates rather than days you spend one at a time. Every day
        inside it that you were rostered for pays flat; days you were not rostered for pay
        nothing, because a vacation does not invent shifts you never had. It shows on the
        calendar and in the shift log, and you are never asked to make the hours up.
